# Development and temporal evaluation of prehospital prediction models for nine emergency outcomes: overall and specialty-specific EMT cohorts

Research manuscript update, 7 September 2026. This version integrates completed specialty P1_04 results, all 324 matched current-model intervals, 270 prespecified paired contrasts, both specialty descriptive cohorts, all 27 required best-tree case panels and a real aggregate-analysis reproduction package. Authors' declarations and source-specific clinical judgments are itemized separately. Scientific/formal acceptance of this report remains false.

## Abstract

**Objective.** To develop and temporally evaluate models for nine emergency outcomes using information available before hospital arrival, and to examine independently developed medicine and surgery extensions.

**Design and setting.** Single-centre retrospective linked emergency medical service and hospital records from Taipei Tzu Chi Hospital, with Taipei and New Taipei EMS sources. Development used 2020–2023, temporal validation used 2024 and frozen evaluation used 2025. The existing methods document reports 24,784 development encounters/19,944 patient groups, 7,504 validation encounters/6,600 groups and 9,504 frozen encounters/8,020 groups; final model-specific denominators are retained separately. [Source: method_TW.md; source_id method.]

**Methods.** Patient-disjoint folds, fold-local preprocessing, development-based model and calibration procedures, locked 2024 operating points and immutable model/spec identities governed evaluation. Twelve final model families span clinical scoring, regression, gradient boosting, neural/text models, foundation models and ensembles. OP_NAME-defined medicine/surgery branches were additional independently trained analyses. Frozen results were not used for reselection.

**Results.** The accepted overall rectangle covers 108 outcome/family tasks; specialty research covers 216. Frozen specialty evaluation and its additive delivery passed independent validation. The integrated primary registry has 324 displayed model/outcome/group records and 324 primary direct explanations; it is unchanged by this report. Current validation intervals cover all 324 models, and the 270 prespecified paired contrasts and both specialty descriptive cohorts are complete. A newly completed locked-model P3 2024 sensitivity yielded AUROC 0.922453 overall, 0.921886 after excluding recorded field ETT and 0.918131 after excluding recorded ETT/SGA. Full estimates, denominators and intervals accompany this manuscript. The completed specialty robustness program covers 494 task dispositions. Vital-only AUPRC is descriptively lower than the full-input comparator in all 18 specialty/outcome combinations; corrected time-removal effects vary by outcome. Nine Surgery full-complete-case analyses are not estimable. [Sources: overall_accepted; specialty_research; specialty_fv0; direct324; p3_successor; p3_metrics.]

**Conclusions.** The existing results support description of temporal predictive performance under the locked contracts. They do not establish clinical effectiveness, fairness across all relevant groups or transportability to another hospital. The completed sensitivity program identifies input and population dependence, with rare-event and complete-case limitations. Prospective clinical benefit is not established.

## Introduction

Prehospital emergency records contain physiological measurements, clinical assessments and treatments collected before hospital arrival. This study evaluates whether such information supports prediction of mortality, airway-related outcomes, admission and prolonged stay. Its comparison spans clinical scores and several model families within a common temporal framework. Predictions could inform further research on early risk assessment; this study does not evaluate whether acting on model outputs improves patient outcomes.

The specialty extension addresses a distinct research question: how independently developed models behave in OP_NAME-defined medicine and surgery cohorts. It is not an audit that applies one overall winner to two subgroups. Because the specialty extension was added after overall frozen evaluation, the chronology is explicitly disclosed. A prospective Spanish ALS cohort studied NEWS2 for early in-hospital mortality, whereas an unselected Danish ambulance cohort found only moderate performance for commonly used early-warning scores. These differing populations and time horizons motivate the present comparisons but do not provide a directly comparable AUPRC target. [Martín-Rodríguez et al., 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32169257/); [Lindskou et al., 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10413164/).

### Outcomes and prediction time

The nine binary outcomes are P1 emergency-department mortality, P2 hospital mortality, P3 the recorded ENDO-related intubation outcome, S1/S2 mortality within three/seven days, S3 specified observation/ward admission dispositions, S4 ICU admission, S5 ICU stay ≥14 days and S6 total hospital stay ≥30 days. Mortality-equivalent critical discharge remains in P1/P2 under the current method. Missing ENDO maps to negative under the locked definition; missing hospital days for S1/S2 derivation maps to zero. The exact source codes, time horizons and missing-outcome rules are in the bound methods/specs and must accompany submission; broad explorer labels are not operational definitions. [Sources: method; overall_accepted.]

The index is before hospital arrival. Arrival vitals and unapproved hospital-derived variables are excluded from the primary predictor allowlist. Authorized prehospital interventions, including airway care, remain legitimate recorded predictors under the study definition. Their inclusion does not establish causal direction or certainty of the outcome. +ED-early and congestion analyses are separately labelled comparisons. [Sources: method_qa; stage1a_manifest; effective-method addendum.]

## Discussion

The study's completed primary evidence supports describing predictive performance within the studied setting and locked temporal design. It does not demonstrate improved care, safety of treatment decisions or broad external validity. Outcome prevalence and model-specific complete-component exclusions affect interpretation, particularly for rSI-sMS. The revised final324/v1 comparison documents changed display scope and differing cohorts; its deltas cannot establish that newer models improved because all 162 shared-family 2024 denominators differ. [Source: final324_vs_v1_comparison.json.]

Surgery/P1 had very few development events, and its lack of a valid representative should remain visible. The current specialty report records nine surgery versus 129 medicine development P1 events. Calibration instability, sample-size adequacy and uncertainty require outcome-specific appraisal; a generic event-per-variable threshold is not evidence of sufficiency for every neural or ensemble model. [Source: specialty_report.]

Further limitations include retrospective single-centre data, city/source differences, missingness, measurement and outcome ascertainment error, mortality-equivalent critical discharge, historical 2025 exposure and the additional-analysis chronology. The specialty router's hospital provenance constrains what “prehospital specialty prediction” can mean operationally. The completed specialty sensitivity results above strengthen the need for outcome-specific interpretation; they do not remove those sources of bias.
