# Primary-source literature context

Prehospital risk scores are useful comparators, but cohort selection and outcome horizon materially affect apparent performance. A prospective Spanish ALS cohort evaluated prehospital NEWS2 for early in-hospital mortality; a large Danish study instead assessed routinely used early-warning scores in an unselected ambulance population and reported moderate performance. These studies support examining both conventional scores and richer predictors; they do not provide a directly comparable AUPRC target for this single-centre linked cohort or its nine different outcomes. [Martín-Rodríguez et al., 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32169257/); [Lindskou et al., 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10413164/).

Decision-curve analysis explicitly links a threshold probability to the relative consequences of false-positive and false-negative decisions. Thus a higher AUROC alone does not establish greater clinical usefulness, and a study threshold must be distinguished from an implemented treatment policy. The current report preserves recorded thresholds and net benefit, without claiming a trial of clinical benefit. [Vickers and Elkin, 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17099194/).

TRIPOD+AI provides a reporting framework across regression and machine-learning prediction studies; it is not a certification of low bias or clinical effectiveness. This report uses its structure to separate data, model development, evaluation, open-science material and unconfirmed author declarations. [Collins et al., 2024](https://www.bmj.com/content/385/bmj-2023-078378).
